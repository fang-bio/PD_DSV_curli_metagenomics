# Main figures

This folder contains the main figures associated with the manuscript:

**Multi-cohort metagenomic analysis identifies prevalence-driven Desulfovibrio–curli co-enrichment and functional reorganization in Parkinson’s disease**

Fang Chi, Yue Yin, Shuai Ji, Tommi Vatanen, and Per E. J. Saris

The main figures summarize the multi-cohort metagenomic meta-analysis, targeted microbial feature associations, ecological and risk interactions between Desulfovibrio and E. coli curli genes, and functional pathway-level reorganization associated with Desulfovibrio–curli co-elevation.

---

## Figure 1

**A multi-cohort meta-analysis of fecal metagenomes in Parkinson’s disease**

- **Panel a:** Geographic distribution of the 1,609 fecal metagenomes included in this study and sample numbers per cohort after quality control.
- **Panel b:** Principal coordinates analysis (PCoA) of gut microbial community composition based on Aitchison distance for Parkinson’s disease patients and healthy controls.
- **Panel c:** Targeted association analysis of Desulfovibrio, Escherichia coli, and E. coli curli genes using a MaAsLin3 mixed-effects framework. The analysis assessed both prevalence-level and abundance-level associations with Parkinson’s disease.
- **Panel d:** Classical abundance validation of Desulfovibrio, Escherichia coli, and E. coli curli genes between Parkinson’s disease patients and healthy controls. Relative abundances were visualized after log10 transformation with a pseudocount of 10⁻⁶. Group differences were assessed using two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum tests.

---

## Figure 2

**Ecological and risk interactions between Desulfovibrio and bacterial curli genes**

- **Panel a:** Spearman’s rank correlations between Desulfovibrio abundance and curli-gene abundance in samples in which both features were detected, stratified by disease status.
- **Panel b:** Spearman’s rank correlations between Desulfovibrio abundance and total Escherichia coli abundance in Parkinson’s disease patients and healthy controls.
- **Panel c:** Forest plot of odds ratios for interaction terms from mixed-effects logistic regression models, including Desulfovibrio × curli genes and Desulfovibrio × total Escherichia coli. Odds ratios are shown per joint standard deviation increase, and error bars indicate 95% confidence intervals.
- **Panel d:** Predicted Parkinson’s disease probability surface derived from the mixed-effects logistic regression model as a function of standardized Desulfovibrio abundance and curli-gene abundance.

Spearman’s P values are shown for correlation analyses. Mixed-effects logistic regression models included cohort identity as a random effect.

---

## Figure 3

**Functional profile differences and pathway-level features associated with Desulfovibrio–curli co-elevation**

- **Panel a:** Principal coordinates analysis (PCoA) of metagenomic functional profiles based on Aitchison distance. Samples were stratified into four Desulfovibrio–curli exposure groups: Both Low, Curli Only, Desulfovibrio Only, and Both High. Ellipses denote 80% confidence intervals. PERMANOVA results for the Desulfovibrio × curli interaction term are shown.
- **Panel b:** Diverging lollipop plot showing pathway-level interaction coefficients from multivariable interaction models. Pathways are ordered by interaction coefficient (β), with point size indicating statistical significance as −log10 q value. q values were derived from multiple-testing correction.
- **Panel c:** Heatmap of group-mean standardized pathway abundances, shown as z-scores, for representative pathways with positive Desulfovibrio × curli interaction coefficients across the four exposure groups.
- **Panel d:** Heatmap of group-mean standardized pathway abundances, shown as z-scores, for representative pathways with negative Desulfovibrio × curli interaction coefficients across the four exposure groups.

## Notes

The main figures were generated using the analysis scripts provided in the repository. These figures support the major findings of the study, including prevalence-driven enrichment of Desulfovibrio, Escherichia coli, and E. coli curli genes in Parkinson’s disease; disease-specific Desulfovibrio–curli ecological coupling; interaction-associated Parkinson’s disease risk; and functional pathway-level reorganization associated with Desulfovibrio–curli co-elevation.
